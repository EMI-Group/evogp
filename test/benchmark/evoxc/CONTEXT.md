# `test/benchmark/evoxc/` — evoxc engine vs plain-PyTorch controlled harness

## Intent
Answer one question with evidence: what does the evoxc-based EvoGP engine cost per evaluation compared with the plain-PyTorch implementation of the same tree evaluation, on identical populations, in the same session, warm/steady-state.
The final campaign widens that question to the post-optimization tree: HEAD against the pre-optimization snapshot `530518e` measured in one window, plus a cold-start column and whole-training-run columns; `FINAL.md` is the report of record.
A second round prices the opt-in `batch` route (`EVOGP_EVAL_MODE=batch`, one merged graph per population structure multiset) against the same arms, on a fixed population and inside a real `StandardPipeline.step()` loop; `BATCH.md` is its report of record.
It is the "new engine vs old torch version" counterpart to `../r4/`/`../r5/`/`../r6/`, which compare the evoxc engine against the deleted legacy CUDA engine; those rounds are frozen history and are never edited from here.
Every arm is validated numerically against `src/evogp/tests/reference_eval.py` before its timings are trusted (`VALIDATION.md`), and every record states the environment, the route that actually resolved, and the cold compile it paid.
A batch-route record also splits one evaluation into ten stages (`stages.py`), so it states where the time went — host decode/dedupe/trace/merge versus lower/compile/load versus upload/run/gather — and not only how much of it there was.

## Constraints
- One process per timed config (the engine's compile cache, executable memo and input memo are per-process), and never two timed configs concurrently.
- `cold_start.py` measures one population per process for the same reason: a `pop` loop inside one process would be cold only for its first population (use `--pop` and one process per value).
- `training_run.py` drives `StandardPipeline.step()` instead of `run()` (which is the same loop plus the stopping rules) so each generation is a timed unit; the fitness callable still goes through `problem.evaluate`. Arms share one seed and one problem construction, and speed is only read next to `best_fitness_by_generation`.
- `auto` resolves to dense+iree at `pop >= 64` on a device box and to specialized+numpy below that, on a CPU box, or on any fallback (`EVOGP_DENSE_AUTO_MIN_POP` moves the threshold, `EVOGP_DENSE_AUTO_MAX_ROWS` the rows ceiling), so the `evoxc-auto` arm measures the dense path at pop ≥ 64 and the specialized path at pop 16.
- `before` carries no torch arms by design: the torch baselines are tree-root-independent, so their after values are the reference for both sides.
- The gate of record is per side — `python3 check.py --results results/final/after --allow-snapshot --allow-missing --allow-digest-mismatch` (130 records / 3602 checks) or `--results results/final/before` (90 records / 2642 checks) — because `results/final/cold/` and `results/final/training/` hold a different schema so the container directory is not gateable in one run, and because the 10 unscheduled `evoxc-specialized-iree` cells and the numpy-vs-iree digest split are declared expectations rather than defects.
- Never run anything else that imports torch while a campaign is in flight — the box has one usable device and the numbers are the deliverable.
- Cold compile never sits in the warm numbers: `worker.py` times the first call separately (`first_call_ms`, `cache.compile_misses_first_call`) before the warmup loop.
- `specialized`-iree costs ~272-284 ms of compile per distinct tree structure on this box, so that arm is scoped to `pop ∈ {16, 64}`; the dropped cells are listed in `campaign.json` (`protocol.scope_dropped`) instead of being silently omitted.
- `EVOGP_EVAL_MODE=batch` is the third evaluation route (`evogp/tree/batch_engine.py`): ONE merged graph per population (structure multiset) built on `evoxc.batch_functions`, iree with a numpy fallback like dense, and `auto` never resolves to it. Its knobs are single-sourced in `common.BATCH_KNOBS` (documented defaults in the root CONTEXT): `EVOGP_BATCH_BAND_SIZE` 16 programs per band (`K = ceil(n / size)` over the deduped function count, clamped by the binding policy), `EVOGP_BATCH_BANDS` (an explicit K, `none`/`flat`, or the opt-in `auto` policy the engine resolves and the binding policy clamps), `EVOGP_BATCH_MIN_POP` 64 — below it the route declines to `specialized` — `EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS` (the compiled-graph pool width, 4 by default: a cold-for-warm trade whose break-even is measured at 3 849 / 5 103 warm `sr_fitness` calls of ONE population at `p256-L32` / `p256-L64` and 18 702 (min-based 20 648) at `p256-L128-i32-N1024-d7`, so an explicit `=1` is the escape hatch for a fixed multiset evaluated more often than that, never a new default) and `EVOGP_BATCH_CHUNK_BANDS` (the banded+chunked composition opt-in, off; the `batch-auto-chunked`/`batch-b16-chunked` routes turn it on). With the shipped defaults (band size 16, pool 4, `CHUNK_BANDS` off) a pop-256 plan is a 4-chunk plan (n = 225–245 deduped functions ≥ `MIN_CHUNK_FUNCTIONS` 32), i.e. the shipped default's measured column is the `b16-cw4` composition while the `EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS=1` rows are its single-body control, and at `p64-L64-i32-N64` (n = 63) chunking is inert on the loss and `batch_forward` plans.
- The per-stage split is a SECOND, optional counter contract on the same module (`batch_engine.batch_stage_stats()` / `batch_stage_reset()`), resolved through `stages.stage_stats_fn`/`stage_reset_fn` and recorded as windows (`batch_cache.stage_*` in `worker.py`, `first_call`/`sr_fitness`/`generations[i]`/`summary` stage blocks in `batch_economics.py`). Null, never zero: an absent accessor, a missing key or a half-read window is `None` (or `available: false`) plus a note, and `batch_<stage>_max_ms` is the after snapshot's PROCESS-LIFETIME maximum, not a delta. Without `batch_stage_reset()` every block stays cumulative, which is why the records read before -> after deltas instead of per-call blocks.
- The BAND knob is swept by `band_sweep.py` (one child process per `(cell, config)`): the band-partition configs (`b8|b16|b32|b64|flat|auto|k15|k7`) pin `EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS=1`, so a difference between two of them is the band partition alone, while the composition configs (`auto-cw4|b16-cw4|flat-cw4|k15-cw4|k7-cw4`) set the pool themselves and answer the banded+chunked / flat-chunked question instead. A record's pool width is its own config env (`EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS`, rendered in the record), and a pure pool-width sweep stays `batch_economics.py --knob EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS=N`.
- The round-3 `k*` pins are the only configs with a POPULATION FLOOR (`pop >= 8 * K`: `k15*` needs 120, `k7*` needs 56), because an explicit `EVOGP_BATCH_BANDS=K` is refused by the engine rather than clamped; `k15*` is therefore legal only on the pop-256-or-larger cells (the p64 cell keeps `k7*`), and a chunked pin is a GLOBAL count split proportionally per chunk (4 chunks -> ~4 bands each at `K=15`, ~2 at `K=7`), so "K per chunk at the cap" is not expressible through `EVOGP_BATCH_BANDS` at all. Cells need no registration: `--cells` goes through `common.parse_cell`, so `p1000-L32-i8-N1024` (the round-3 population column) is runnable as-is.
The population, the data and the seed are identical across configs, so what two records of one cell must share is the ORACLE deviation (`validation.<workload>.max_abs/max_rel/bitwise_equal`), which is config-independent — measured exactly equal in all five configs of every cell/workload.
Value digests are a weaker witness and split by design: `sr_fitness` is bitwise identical across the banded configs and differs only on the flat merge (1 ulp at `p256-L32`), while `batch_forward`/`forward` form per-config digest groups at ≤5e-11 relative `abs_sum` — the same order as the route's documented non-bitwise `batch_forward` caveat, not a band-size accuracy effect.
- The dense route's trace cap is `EVOGP_DENSE_MAX_TREE_LEN` (`dense_engine.MAX_TREE_LEN_ENV`, default `dense_engine.DEFAULT_MAX_TREE_LEN` = 64): when `tree_len_max > min(EVOGP_DENSE_MAX_TREE_LEN, forest.max_tree_len)` the dense evaluator raises `DenseUnsupported` and the public API falls back to the per-tree `specialized` route — silently. `evoxc-dense-iree` measures that fallback on a long-tree cell, `evoxc-dense-iree-raised` pins the cap to the cell's `{tree_len}` and measures dense itself, and the explicit-dense record's `dense_route` says which happened; the long-tree cells (`--cells long`, `max_tree_len` 128/256) exist to price that fallback against the batch route, whose per-program cost scales with the merge rather than with the cap.
  Tree length has two axes on those cells: the actual node count of the population is `max_layer_cnt`-driven (a full tree is `2 ** d − 1` nodes: seed 0 / pop 64 gives mean/max 15.8/29 at `d=5`, 27.1/57 at `d=6`, 35.5/101 at `d=7`, 69.9/187 at `d=8`), while the dense trace length is `tree_len`-driven (the row stride, and the value the cap is compared against), so a long-tree cell pins both and the `-d<k>` sibling cells separate the two axes.
- A batch arm's large `first_call_ms` and large `compile_misses_first_call` are the measurement, not a defect: the merged graph covers the whole population, so the cold call pays one multi-second-to-minutes compile, which the existing one-untimed-first-call protocol keeps out of the warm medians; `check.py` therefore has no upper bound on either number and only WARNs when the compile is not attributed to any counter.
- The batch arms carry no `campaign.SCOPE` rule: they are measured on every cell a round schedules, with `--timeout` bounding a runaway config (an overrun is recorded as `status: "timeout"` with its worker log as evidence).
  On the single-executable compile path (`EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS=1`, the control arm) the measured merged compile is 10 725.86 / 11 957.38 ms per workload at `p256-L32-i8-N1024` / `p256-L64-i32-N1024`, 38 513.95 ms at the long-tree cell `p256-L128-i32-N1024-d7`, and 91.0–162.0 s at pop 1000 (91.1 s at p1000-L32-i8-N1024 and 162.0 s at p1000-L64-i32-N1024 in the gateable `results/batch/warm-r5` records, 166.9 s in the `--reps 3` pilot `results/batch/warm`), plus 102.7 s for a flat (K=1) merge at pop 1000.
  The shipped default (band size 16, `EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS` 4) compiles those pop-256 plans cold in 4 064.48 / 4 071.43 ms (2.64× / 2.94× cheaper than pool 1) at 1.69–1.87× the warm single-body call (1.9941 → 3.7250, 2.2267 → 3.7720 ms), and chunking is inert below 32 deduped functions, so the pop-64 cells stay on the single-body path (2 274.46 ms cold at `p64-L64-i32-N64`, its pool-1 twin 2 268.96 ms).
  `BATCH.md` §13/§15, `results/batch_opt_round1/SUMMARY.md` and `results/batch_opt_round3/SUMMARY.md` carry these numbers.
- A timed workload needs `--reps >= 5`: `check.py` enforces `len(samples_ms) == reps >= MIN_SAMPLES` (5), so a `--reps 3` campaign is structurally ungateable and must be reported as a pilot, not as evidence.
- Records are written verbatim: a contaminated or re-run cell is annotated (`--note`, guard block), never smoothed.
- `results/baseline/`, `results/final/**`, `results/batch/**`, `results/batch_opt_round1/` and `results/batch_opt_round2/` are frozen evidence (both batch drivers refuse them as `--out-dir`); a re-run writes into a fresh result directory, and `../r2/`, `../r4/`, `../r5/`, `../r6/` are never touched from here.
- This subtree is not collected by pytest and is not part of the `src/evogp/tests/` suite.

## Adding a new arm (an additional evaluation route)
Registration is data, not code: an evoxc-style route is one entry in `common.EVOXC_ARMS` carrying `{"eval_mode", "backend", "family"}` plus an optional `"knobs"` mapping (env name -> value, e.g. `evoxc-batch-iree-flat`'s `EVOGP_BATCH_BANDS=flat`, or a per-cell template such as `evoxc-dense-iree-raised`'s `EVOGP_DENSE_MAX_TREE_LEN={tree_len}`), `None` meaning "leave the env var unset".
`common.ALL_ARMS` is derived, so `--arms all|evoxc` and `check.py`'s expected-arm coverage pick it up with no further edit; append new arms LAST, because registry order is the render order used by `summarize.order_arms` and the `auto route` column's fallback.
`arms.apply_arm_env(arm, cell=None)` writes `EVOGP_EVAL_MODE`/`EVOGP_EVOXC_BACKEND` from that spec and then the spec's `knobs`, resolved through `arms.resolve_knob_value` against the cell dict (it clears every `common.ROUTE_ENV_VARS` entry first for every arm, and `KeyError`s on an unregistered name).
Arm methods take no arguments (`getattr(arm_obj, name)()` in `worker.py`), and the object must expose `setup_ms`, `family` and `fitness(forest, report=None)`; a route that needs a different call path needs its own class in `arms.py` (the batch arms do not: they go through the same public `Forest.SR_fitness`/`batch_forward`/`forward`).
`worker.py` reads compile economics through `evoxc_engine.cache_stats()` and, additionally for a batch arm, through `evogp.tree.batch_engine.batch_cache_stats` when that module exposes it — a new route whose counters live elsewhere must be pointed at its own stats callable (null, never 0, when it is missing). The same holds for the per-stage split: a new route's stage accessors are registered in `stages.py` (`STATS_FN_NAME`/`RESET_FN_NAME`, the one owner of the ten-stage vocabulary and of every derived block), and its costs then render as the null-or-measured blocks `worker.py` and `batch_economics.py` already write.
`check.py` treats any arm absent from `TORCH_ARMS` as an evoxc arm, so a new arm is required to record `routes` plus a non-`None` `cache.compile_misses_first_call`, and its `batch_forward`/`forward` sha256 and every workload's `nan`/`finite` counts must match the other arms of the cell (or be declared with `--allow-digest-mismatch`); `campaign.SCOPE` is the only per-arm coverage rule, and the batch arms have none because their round scheduled them explicitly on the cells that matter (`BATCH.md` §6 has the compile numbers a scoping rule would be built from).
A route with extra knobs should route them through `common.BATCH_*`-style single-sourced tables and record its own resolved policy block, the way a batch arm records `batch_policy`; `check_batch` is the model for the checks such a route deserves (mode support + route identity as FAIL, counter attribution as WARN).
A route that only differences its ENV (a band policy, a pool width, the banded+chunked opt-in) needs no new registry arm at all: it is one `ROUTE_SPECS` entry in `batch_economics.py` (`{"arm", "knobs"}` — the spec is validated against `common.ROUTE_ENV_VARS` when the token is resolved, and an explicit `--knob` overrides it for the same variable), and `protocol.route_knobs` then states which layer won.
`summarize.py` renders any record it finds (`order_arms` puts an unregistered arm last), derives the route kind from the record's own `routes[...]kind` when present, and sources the `auto route` column from `evoxc-auto`/`evoxc-auto-iree` first.
`cold_start.py` (default route only, `apply_arm_env("evoxc-auto")`), `training_run.py` (its own `ARMS = ("engine", "torch-per-tree", "preopt")`), `decompose.py` (its own `ROUTES` table, keyed on route name + instrumented phase lists) and `batch_economics.py` (its own `ROUTE_SPECS` table: `batch`/`batch-flat`/`dense` plus the knob-spec routes `batch-banded`/`batch-b16-banded`/`batch-flat-chunked`/`batch-auto-chunked`/`batch-b16-chunked`) do NOT read the arm registry, so a new route needs a separate edit in each to be measured there.

## Known Issues
- The `EVOGP_BATCH_BANDS=auto` band policy is neither accurate nor stable, and the legality policy is what usually decides: ROCm/iree binds `{backend iree, device_kind rocm, max_bands 15, min_band_size 8, uncapped false}`, so a request above 15 is clamped and every band needs `>= 8` functions. It overshoots 14.2–14.7× on the loss plans at pop 256 (`band_requested_bands` 213/220, `bands` 15, `band_clamped true`) and undershoots to 4 bands on the per-row `forward` plan there and to a bare 1 — the flat merge — at pop 64 (`cost_estimate == flat_estimate` 321984), where the loss plan asks 16 and is served 7 (`band_sizes` `[9]*7`); the answer is not population-stable either (`p256-L32-i8-N1024`, `population_signature` `ad11cb66…`, 225 functions reads K = 213 in its sweep record and K = 1 in the generation-loop first call, and the records of one cell carry K = 1, 4 and 213 together), so there is no single "auto policy" to reason about. It lost all 9 warm columns of its grid (slowest of three configs in 7 of 9, inside the 2.6–6 % noise band in only 4, worst +18.9 % on the per-row `forward` at `p256-L32`; cold 1.12–1.35× the cheapest at the pop-256 cells, 1.01–1.11× at pop 64) and its clamp is not free: +0.18–1.11 s of cold host merge per call, 4.90–5.35× the merge that reaches the same final band shape, inside one `merge` timer block (`batch_merge_calls` stays 1). Evidence: `results/batch_opt_round2/{auto,composition,smoke-cold}/**`.
- `EVOGP_BATCH_BANDS=none` (flat) does NOT chunk even at `EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS=4`: the plan reports `compile_workers 1` / `chunk_count 1` / `chunk_bands [1]`, because the chunk gate (`src/evogp/tree/batch_programs.py::_resolve_workers`) refuses an explicit `EVOGP_BATCH_BANDS` spelling — `K`, `none`/`flat` or `auto` — unless `EVOGP_BATCH_CHUNK_BANDS` opts in, while a band SIZE does not refuse chunking (the shipped default chunks; see the `EVOGP_EVAL_MODE=batch` knob line in Constraints), so `EVOGP_BATCH_CHUNK_BANDS=1` is the only way to chunk an explicit band request.
- Banded+chunked composition (`EVOGP_BATCH_CHUNK_BANDS=1` + pool 4, band size pinned) is a COLD lever only: at the two pop-256 cells it buys 2.64× / 2.94× on cold (10 725.86 → 4 064.48, 11 957.38 → 4 071.43 ms) and costs 1.69× / 1.87× on warm `sr_fitness` (1.9941 → 3.7250, 2.2267 → 3.7720; four separate upload/run/read-back trios per call, `run`/`upload`/`gather` all up), so break-even is 3 849 / 5 103 warm calls of ONE population (min-based 3 981 / 5 083); it is inert at `p64-L64-i32-N64` on the loss and `batch_forward` plans (`chunk_count` 1, `chunk_sizes [63]`, 2 274.46 against 2 268.96 ms) while the per-row plan there still splits 2-way. Evidence: `results/batch_opt_round3/economics/item1/`, `BATCH.md` §15.2.
- Every batch route remains 17.8–71.5× behind `dense` over an 8-generation loop (64.9× / 71.5× / 17.8× at `p256-L32-i8-N1024` / `p256-L64-i32-N1024` / `p64-L64-i32-N64`: 19 626.71 / 22 346.79 / 5 587.44 ms against 302.48 / 312.48 / 313.26, and an eval-only ratio of 868 / 680 / 229×), because every generation presents a new structure multiset (7 of 8 generations compile, 8 distinct signatures in 8 steps) and chunking only splits that one compile 4 ways — it never amortizes a loop.
- `batch_economics.py` has no idle-device guard: its `economics.json` carries no `guard` block, so a loop run's idleness must be verified by hand with `rocm-smi` and the runs kept strictly sequential.
- The band-policy decision is `EVOGP_BATCH_BAND_SIZE` = 16 as a minimax call, `EVOGP_BATCH_BANDS=auto` opt-in only and `EVOGP_BATCH_CHUNK_BANDS` off; the band size is the second-order knob (3–6 % between `b8/b16/b32/b64`, at this box's cross-config noise floor) and flat-vs-banded is the first-order decision: at `EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS=1` (`results/batch_opt_round2/warm/SUMMARY.md`) a single unbanded merge (K = 1) is 15 % slower on warm `sr_fitness`, 8 % on `batch_forward` and 34 % on the per-row `forward` at the two pop-256 cells (worst column +53 %), while the whole spread among the banded configs (`b8/b16/b32/b64` → K = 29–31/15–16/8/4) is 3–6 % (the same-shape `p64` twin b64-vs-flat reads 2.6 %).
  Band size 16 is the minimax setting on both the median- and the min-based estimator (warm-sweep geomean 1.0185 of the per-column argmin, worst column 1.061 there / 1.102 pooled over both rounds, against 1.0223/1.137 for 32 and 1.0415/1.167 for 8), and it is where a `K ≤ 15` policy clamp lands anyway; 32 gives up 12–14 % on the band-sensitive `forward` at `p256-L32-i8-N1024`.
  Where the band size does act, it is the `run` stage (`forward` `run` 0.232 ms at K = 16 vs 0.589 at K = 1) — `upload` and `gather` are band-count-insensitive, and the flat merge's `sr_fitness` `upload` alone doubles at `p256-L64-i32-N1024`; the `p64-L64-i32-N64` cell is inert (all five configs within 6 %).
  Pooled over the warm sweep and the `auto` round (18 columns) the call is a dead heat on the mean — median-based geomean of the per-column argmin `b16` 1.0248 vs `b32` 1.0224, min-based `b16` 1.0199 vs `b32` 1.0254 — and is decided on the worst column: `b16`'s worst is 1.102 (min-based 1.109) against `b32`'s 1.143 (1.137), which is also where the `K <= 15` clamp lands. The `b16` argmin is a power-of-two grid result on three cells, no band-size grid covers `p1000`, and `bands=auto` stays opt-in.
  The cap pin is measured, not inferred: `EVOGP_BATCH_BANDS=15` at the rocm binding cap reproduces the shipped size's merge exactly at pop 256 (identical `band_sizes` and identical `emitted_ops` — the size model is merely clamped to 15 on the per-row plan while the pin asks the count) and at `p64` it does change the plan (K 4 `[16,16,16,15]` → K 7 `[9]×7`, `emitted_ops` 1 393 → 1 463) yet lands inside this box's ~5 % cross-config noise; no pin beat the shipped size anywhere in the window (the pop-256 columns are one merge read twice), and the partition is a scheduling decision rather than a numeric one — the oracle deviation is exactly identical in all four configs of every cell/workload (9/9 rows) and the value digests split only at ≤5.8e-11 relative `abs_sum`, by pool at pop 256 and by band count on `p64 batch_forward` (`results/batch_opt_round3/item3/`).
- The batch route is not uniformly dominated: warm `sr_fitness` on trees that fit the shipped dense cap is 3.38× / 1.84× dearer than `dense+iree` at the two pop-256 cells but 1.29× faster at `p64` (1.1141 vs 1.4378 ms), and its cold compile is cheaper than dense's at `p64` while dearer at pop 256 (see the compile numbers in Constraints); a generation loop rebuilds the merged graph almost every generation (7 of 8, 0.951–0.995 compile share) with 2 600.93 / 2 894.58 / 588.22 ms of eval per recompiling generation against dense's whole-generation 2.9–4.2 ms; once the population's longest tree exceeds the default cap of 64 the ordering flips — batch is 1.5–4.2× faster than dense with `EVOGP_DENSE_MAX_TREE_LEN` raised to cover the population (need-capped dense still wins by 1.05–1.41× at long strides) and 23.5–25× faster than the cap-64 decline fallback (per-tree `specialized`) — while a loop still loses (the `p256-L128-i32-N1024-d7` second generation recompiles: 3 933.60 ms chunked against 8 601.53 ms single-body, 2.19×, next to ~3 ms of operator work). Its other win is the fixed population of ~64 trees at N=64, and even there a generation loop loses. `BATCH.md` is the report of record (§11 for the long-tree round, §15 for the shipped-default column), `results/batch/**` and `results/batch_opt_round3/**` the evidence.
- The content-addressed plan key wins only for a rebuilt-but-equal population: at `p64-L64-i32-N64` a second `Forest` built from the same seed evaluates in 2.724 ms against the pre-round tree's 79.109 ms (29.0×, 76.4 ms of host decode + trace + merge removed), with `batch_content_hits` 1 / 0 misses / `hit_rate` 1.0, trace/merge/compile stage calls 0 and a bit-identical answer (sha256 `b6584500…`), while the pre-round tree pays a plan miss and re-decodes + re-traces + re-merges (its exec table already shares the compiled body, so the 76.4 ms is host-side work, never a recompile). In a generation loop it buys nothing — 6 generations at the same cell read 6 attempts / 0 hits, `{rows 6, with_content_hit 0, with_content_miss 5, no_attempt 1}` — because a generation presents new programs every step, so the rebuilt-object regime `reuse_probe.py` measures stays the only one it helps. `BATCH.md` §13.10 is its report of record.
- The long-tree d7/d8 cells split into four sha256 groups per value workload and need `--allow-digest-mismatch`: `specialized`+iree contracts a `mul` into the following `add`/`sub` (FMA, ≤2.2e-3 relative at d7 and ≤6.1e-3 at d8 after deep-tree cancellation, minimal 5-node repro `add(mul(x0,x1), x0)`), while dense differs from numpy only in the NaN payload bit on `sub` and is value-identical to `specialized`-numpy and `torch-per-tree`; the pop-1000 cell splits into three groups (both dense caps share one).
- `EVOGP_DENSE_MAX_TREE_LEN` is a cost knob, not only a safety valve: pinning it above what the population needs multiplies dense's per-call cost (2.030 → 5.277 ms at 31-node trees for cap 64 → 128, 2.126 → 19.497 ms for 64 → 256), so the `evoxc-dense-iree-raised` arm may only be compared inside its own cell and the shipped default of 64 stays.
- The batch `batch_forward` value path is non-bitwise on the `arith` set where dense is exact (1.5625e-02 abs / 1.13e-03 rel at p256-L32-i8-N1024, 7.81e-02 abs at p256-L64-i32-N1024; the loss and per-row paths agree to ~1e-7 rel), which is why the batch arm forms its own sha256 digest group; `results/batch/validation-batch.json` vs `validation-dense.json` is the comparison.
- Explicit `EVOGP_EVAL_MODE=dense` at pop 16 recompiles when the population's used-function-set changes (2 compiles in 3 generations, ~3.0 s each, `gp_step` 3015.8 ms vs 8.0 ms on `auto`, which routes pop < 64 to specialized) — engine behaviour, recorded in `results/batch/small-r5/p16-L32-i8-N1024/evoxc-dense-iree.json`.
- Asking for the batch route below the population floor costs ≈1.6 ms per call more than asking `specialized` directly (3.94 ms vs 2.31 ms at pop 16), so the decline path is not free.
- `batch_economics.py` and `validate.py` have now both been exercised on the box (economics on 4 cells × 2 routes, validation on 2 cells × 3 function sets with `EVOGP_EVAL_MODE=batch`), but `validate.py` still has no batch entry of its own — the batch sweep is produced by exporting the route, not by a named set.
- `results/batch/flat1000-r5` cannot reach 0 failures under its own `--workloads sr_fitness` schedule (`check.py` wants a digest for every value workload in the cell); the 3-workload sibling `flat1000-3w-r5` is the gateable record of that cell.
- The guard's `python` hint field lists the harness's own `systemd-run` launcher chain, not a foreign tenant; `contaminated` stays false and the gate reads `ok`/`idle` on all 220 records (130/130 after, 90/90 before), with empty `gpu_pids`.
- Run without the three allow flags, the gate reports the declared expectations as failures: 38 on `results/final/after` and 39 on `results/baseline` (the 10 unscheduled `evoxc-specialized-iree` cells plus the numpy-vs-iree sha256 split on `forward`/`batch_forward`); pointing it at the container `results/final` fails on the `cold/`, `training/` and `decompose*/` subdirectories' different schema.
- The `etl`-shadow halves of R2/R3/R4 remain non-re-runnable (their session-scoped shadow exports are gone); the committed records and per-log probe lines are the surviving evidence.
- The committed `results/batch/**` records predate the per-stage split, the `compile_workers`/`chunk_count`/`chunk_sizes` plan fields and the `EVOGP_BATCH_COMPILE_WORKERS` sweep (they carry no `stage_*` keys at all — absent, not null), so the later measurement round re-measures those cells instead of back-filling them.
- `batch_economics.py` records the exec table's misses per generation (`batch_compile_misses`) next to the plan table's (`batch_plan_misses`), and its records carry the `evogp-bench-evoxc-v1-batch-economics` schema, so `check.py` cannot gate a `batch_economics` directory or a directory that contains one (it reads each `economics.json` as an arm record and fails on the missing `env`/workload fields) — the gate unit for such a round is its campaign-format subdirectory (`results/batch_opt_round1/campaign`, 3 records / 88 checks / 0 failures).
- Coverage limits: `evoxc-specialized-iree` is measured on pop ∈ {16, 64} only (272-284 ms of compile per tree structure makes a dense-scale cell unaffordable), and no `CustomLoss`/multi-output or robotics workload is validated or timed.
